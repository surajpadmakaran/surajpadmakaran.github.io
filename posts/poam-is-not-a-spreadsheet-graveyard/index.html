Your POA&M Is Not a Spreadsheet Graveyard

Plans of Action & Milestones die the same way everywhere: a workbook nobody trusts, updated in a panic before the auditor arrives. It doesn't have to work that way.

Oct 7, 20267 min readGRCBy Suraj Padmakaran

Ask a compliance team where their POA&M lives and watch the room go quiet. Somewhere there's a spreadsheet — probably several, probably contradictory — last touched in a fluorescent panic the week before an assessment. Findings go in. Then they go stale, because updating the workbook is manual, thankless, and nobody's actual job.

The POA&M itself isn't the problem. It's one of the most honest documents in security: what we found, what we'll do about it, by when, and who's accountable. The problem is treating it as a document at all. It should be a system — whether it lives in ServiceNow, a GRC platform, or a well-built database — one that maintains itself.

Automate the bookkeeping, keep the judgment

Here is what I've learned to automate first, in order:

  • Ingestion. Scanner and assessment findings should flow in on their own, deduplicated and mapped to their NIST 800-53 controls. If a human is copy-pasting findings into rows, the process is already broken.
  • Aging and escalation. Due dates don't chase people; systems should. Escalating reminders as deadlines approach — polite at first, then less so — eliminate the 'I never saw it' failure mode entirely.
  • Evidence prompts. When a milestone is marked complete, the system asks for the artifact right then, while it exists. Evidence requested three months later is archaeology.
  • Reporting. If leadership wants a burn-down chart, it should generate itself from live data — not from someone's weekend with pivot tables.

And what should stay human? Risk acceptance. Extension requests. The conversation about whether a compensating control is genuinely compensating. Automation carries the paper; people carry the accountability. Blurring that line in either direction is where programs get hurt.

The test of a living POA&M

Open yours and ask three questions. Is every item's status less than a week old — without anyone having 'updated' it by hand? Could you hand your 3PAO a clean extract in one click, right now, mid-quarter? Does anything in it surprise you?

If the answers are no, no, and yes, you don't have a tracking problem. You have a systems problem wearing a spreadsheet costume. The good news: it's one of the highest-leverage automations in all of GRC, because every hour it saves recurs forever.

— S.P. · Building a POA&M pipeline from scratch? Start with ingestion and aging. Everything else follows.
