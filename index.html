Why this blog exists

the short version

I was on the other side of this once. I was not a security person; I did not know how these attacks worked, and I could have been fooled by the same calls and messages that fool people every day. Now I work in cybersecurity — and this blog is how I bring everyone else across with me.

Most security writing is written for security teams. This one is written for everyone else: for the senior deciding whether that call is really her bank (it almost never is), for the youngster opening a first account, for the family that shares one Wi-Fi network and a dozen passwords. About the author

the short version

Suraj Padmakaran

A cybersecurity practitioner, writing for everyone

By day, I work in cybersecurity — governance, risk and compliance (GRC): helping organisations prove their systems are safe, and building small automations that turn hours of manual checking into minutes. It’s good work, and I’m glad to do it.

But the people attackers actually go after are rarely in those buildings. They’re grandparents answering the phone, youngsters in their first game chat, families just trying to get through the week — and almost nobody explains this stuff to them in words they’d actually use. So that’s what I do here: I take what I know and translate it into plain English. What the scams look like. The few habits that stop most of them. No jargon, no scare tactics, nothing to sell you.

I wasn’t always on this side of it — I once knew none of this, and I could have been an easy target myself. If a chapter here saves one family one bad phone call, it has done its job. There’s a new one every Wednesday. (Fellow practitioners: my technical writing lives under Field Notes.)
